Homelab

This is the real thing — not a mockup. I run two ISPs into the house: Entel over a Huawei ONT that I patched to escape CG-NAT and get a real, reachable IP, and Mundo Pacífico — allegedly the fastest residential fiber in LatAm — on a 10-Gigabit-capable FiberHome ONT, currently running a 2 Gbps plan while I build out the wiring to actually push that through the whole house.

Everything behind those two links goes through a FortiGate 60D I picked up secondhand — no further questions, please — which right now only runs the DMZ, connected to a 24-port TP-Link switch that every server and lab sits on. The architecture is deliberately spread out: an old Core 2 Duo does nothing but reverse-proxy everything through Caddy so no single box is a single point of failure, a Ryzen 5 does the actual heavy lifting, and an old HP ProLiant sits mostly idle as backup muscle. The setup spans two floors: the main rack lives in the central living room, and there's a second, smaller setup upstairs in my bedroom.

Roadmap
  • Done
    CG-NAT bypass on Entel — got a real, reachable public IP on WAN1 instead of sharing one behind carrier NAT.
  • Done
    Split gateway from compute — moved reverse-proxy duty onto its own dedicated box (the ThinkCentre) so the lab server going down doesn't take routing with it.
  • In Progress
    Wiring the house for 2 Gbps — Mundo Pacífico's line can already do it; the internal cabling can't, yet.
  • Planned
    10G/2.5G RJ45 transceiver — off the Mundo Pacífico router's 10G port, to finally give the 2nd-floor rack a real uplink instead of relying on Wi-Fi.
  • Planned
    Expand the bedroom patch panel — to match whatever the transceiver upgrade ends up delivering.
  • Idea
    Segment the DMZ — as the lab grows past "one flat zone," proper VLANs are the obvious next step. Not committed yet.
Dream Homelab
The honest version: I go back and forth between wanting more power and more toys, and knowing full well I might not actually put half of it to good use. This section is that wishlist anyway — the "if money and time were no object" build.
VoIP PBX — a self-hosted Asterisk/FreePBX setup with real extensions across the house, mostly because it sounds like a fun rabbit hole.
Real mesh Wi-Fi — enterprise-grade APs with actual roaming between both floors, instead of range extenders and hope.
A proper network printer — laser plus a label printer, on their own print server, so printing stops being an event.
A mini-PC cluster — a handful of NUC-style boxes running Proxmox, so "La Bestia" stops being the only thing standing between me and downtime.
A dedicated NAS — RAID plus scheduled off-site backups, instead of trusting spinning rust across four different boxes.
Grafana + Prometheus + Uptime Kuma — actual dashboards and alerting instead of finding out something died because someone messaged me.
A real 10G backbone — both floors, both WANs, no bottlenecks — the planned transceiver taken to its logical conclusion.
A proper online double-conversion UPS — with a network card, so shutdowns during a real outage are automatic instead of a phone notification and a sprint.
